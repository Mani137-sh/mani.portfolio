About

I'm a Ph.D. Research Scholar in the Department of Electrical Engineering at IIT Bombay, working on high-performance, low-power hardware for GPS/NavIC acquisition receivers — the piece of a satellite navigation chip that finds and locks onto a signal before anything else can happen.

Before this, I spent two years as an RTL Design Engineer at Chipspirit Technologies, building FPGA-based encryption and decryption systems — writing synthesizable Verilog, generating bitstreams in Vivado, and validating hardware on the bench with ILA/VIO and iperf3.

My work sits across the full stack of chip design: RTL, verification, FPGA prototyping, physical design, and ASIC implementation in Cadence Genus and Innovus — carrying an idea from an algorithm on paper to a synthesized, timing-closed layout.

Research

Since June 2025 — the design of a full acquisition module for a GPS/NavIC receiver chip.

Design of Acquisition Module for the NavIC Receiver Chip

An FSM-based serial-search GPS/NavIC acquisition architecture in Verilog RTL, covering PRN code phases and Doppler bins — the search that lets a receiver find a satellite signal before it can track it.

The datapath includes PRN generation via G1/G2 LFSRs, code-phase rotation, multi-bin Doppler search, MAC-based correlation, and a fixed-point carrier NCO with 0.5° resolution sine/cosine LUTs for carrier generation. Verified through RTL simulation and FPGA prototyping on a PYNQ-Z2 with real input data, then carried to full ASIC implementation in Cadence Genus and Innovus.
